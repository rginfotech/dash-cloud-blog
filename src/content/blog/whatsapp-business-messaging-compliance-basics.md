WhatsApp can be an excellent channel for customer conversations. It also has clear rules, and breaking them can get your number restricted. This article summarises the basics. It is general information, not legal advice. Check the current platform policy and the laws of each country where you operate.

## 1. Get clear permission

Only message people who have agreed to hear from you on WhatsApp. Explain what they will receive and how often, at the point where you collect their number. Keep a record of when and how they agreed.

## 2. Make opting out easy

Every person should be able to stop messages quickly, for example by replying "STOP". Honour it immediately, and do not message them again unless they opt back in.

## 3. Know the message types

- **Customer-initiated conversations:** when someone writes to you, you can reply within the window the platform allows.
- **Business-initiated messages:** these usually need approved templates, and fall into categories such as utility, authentication and marketing.
- Always check the current categories, pricing and rules in the official documentation, as they change.

## 4. Be honest and relevant

Say who you are. Send messages that match what the person signed up for. Avoid misleading wording, and do not send content that is unrelated to the relationship.

## 5. Watch your quality signals

If many people block or report your messages, your quality rating can fall and your sending limits can be reduced. Relevant, infrequent messages protect your number.

## 6. Protect personal data

Collect only what you need. Tell people how their data is used, store it securely, and follow data protection laws such as GDPR where they apply.

## 7. Be clear when AI is replying

If an AI agent is handling the conversation, be open about it, and give an easy route to a human for sensitive matters.

## A quick checklist

- [ ] Consent recorded
- [ ] Opt-out works
- [ ] Templates approved
- [ ] Message frequency reasonable
- [ ] Data stored securely
- [ ] Human handoff available

When in doubt, ask a qualified adviser.
