Shipping an order that was never going to be accepted is one of the most expensive mistakes in D2C. You pay for packaging, outbound freight and often the return trip as well.

## Where the losses come from

- **Wrong or incomplete addresses** that fail delivery
- **Impulse purchases** the buyer reconsiders within hours
- **Cash-on-delivery refusals** where nobody confirmed intent
- **Size, colour or variant mistakes** that a quick check would catch

## A confirmation flow that works

Send a message within minutes of the order. Keep it short and let the buyer reply with one tap.

1. Confirm items, price and delivery address.
2. Ask the buyer to confirm, edit the address, or cancel.
3. For unanswered orders, follow up once, then flag for your team.
4. Pass confirmed orders straight to fulfilment.

## Where AI adds value

A rules-only bot breaks when customers write naturally. An AI agent understands replies like "send it to my office instead" or "can I change the size?", updates the order, and escalates only the exceptions. It also answers delivery and return questions at any hour, which reduces support load.

## What to track

| Metric | Goal |
|---|---|
| Confirmation rate | Share of orders explicitly confirmed |
| Failed delivery rate | Should fall as addresses improve |
| Return rate | Compare before and after, by product |
| Support tickets per order | Should drop |

Test it on one product line or region first, compare against a control group, and expand once the numbers hold.

Use only customers' consented contact details, and follow the WhatsApp Business messaging policy.
