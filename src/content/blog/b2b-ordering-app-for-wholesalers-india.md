# **B2B Ordering App for Wholesalers in India: A Practical Guide (2026)**

If you run a wholesale business in India, you know how the morning goes. By 9 a.m. there are forty WhatsApp messages waiting. One retailer has sent a photo of a handwritten list. Another sent a voice note. Someone wants "same as last time, but 5 cartons less". And at least two people will call later to ask why their rate is different from what the salesman told them.

None of this is a disaster on its own. But it adds up. Orders get missed or typed in wrong. Your staff spend hours copying messages into billing software. Retailers can't see what's in stock, so they order things you don't have. And when your best salesman takes leave, half your order flow goes with him.

A B2B ordering app fixes most of this. Your retailers get your catalogue on their phone, see their own prices, and place orders themselves, any time of day. You get clean orders in one place instead of a chat history.

This guide explains how these apps work, which features actually matter for an Indian wholesaler, what they cost, and how to get your retailers to use one. ## What is a B2B ordering app?

It's an app (and usually a website too) where your business customers order from you. Think of it as your own private online store, but for retailers, shops and institutions rather than the public.

The difference from a normal shopping app is in the details. A kirana owner buying from you doesn't pay the MRP. They pay your trade price, often in cartons or cases, and the price may depend on who they are. A good B2B app handles that without you sending a new rate list every week.

## Features that actually matter

Most software lists run to fifty features. For a wholesaler, these are the ones that make a real difference day to day.

**1. Wholesale pricing.** Retailers should see trade prices, not retail prices. If you have different rates for different buyers, the app should show each buyer their own rate when they log in.

**2. A catalogue they can actually search.** Product photos, pack sizes, brands and categories. A retailer looking for "Tata salt 1 kg" should find it in two taps, not scroll through 800 items.

**3. Quick reorder.** Most B2B orders are repeat orders. If a retailer can open last week's order, change two quantities and send it, they will use the app. If they have to build every order from scratch, they will go back to WhatsApp.

**4. Request a quote.** For bulk or unusual orders, buyers often want to negotiate. A "request a quote" button lets them send what they need and you reply with a price, instead of a long back-and-forth on calls.

**5. Catalogue-only mode.** Some wholesalers don't want to show prices publicly at all. In catalogue mode the app works as a digital product brochure, and orders or enquiries come to you to confirm.

**6. Payment options that match how you work.** Online payment for those who prefer it, and cash on delivery for those who don't. Many wholesale relationships run on credit, so check how the app handles orders that are paid later.

**7. An app for you, not just your buyers.** You or your manager should be able to see new orders, update stock and change prices from your phone, without sitting at the office computer.

**8. Your own brand.** Retailers should download *your* app, with your name and logo, not a third-party marketplace where your competitor is one tap away. This is what a white label app gives you.

**9. GST invoices.** Your buyers need proper GST invoices for their input credit. The app should generate them with every order.

You don't need all of these on day one. Wholesale pricing, quick reorder and a phone app for yourself are the three that decide whether the app gets used.

## Your three options, and what each costs

### 1. List on a B2B marketplace

B2B marketplaces bring you buyers you don't already have, which is useful. But your existing retailers end up ordering inside someone else's platform, next to your competitors' products. You usually pay a commission on each order, and the customer relationship belongs to the marketplace more than to you. Good for finding new buyers, not ideal for serving the ones you already have.

### 2. Get a custom app built

An agency builds exactly what you ask for. For a B2B app with a customer app, an admin panel and a website, expect several lakh rupees to build, plus yearly costs for hosting, updates and fixes. It can take three to six months. This makes sense if your process is truly unusual, or you have a large in-house tech team.

## How to choose

Before you sign up for anything, ask these questions:

* Can each retailer see their own price?
* How fast can a retailer repeat a past order?
* Is there an app for me to manage orders, or only a web dashboard?
* Do my buyers get an app with my name on it?
* What do I pay on each order, and is there a commission?
* What happens to my data if I leave?
* Can I see it working with my own products before I pay?

If a vendor can't answer these clearly in a demo, keep looking.

## Getting retailers to actually use it

This is where most wholesalers struggle. The app works fine, but retailers keep sending WhatsApp messages out of habit. A few things help a lot.

**Start with your best 20 customers.** Don't launch to everyone at once. Pick retailers who order often and are comfortable with phones. Install the app for them during a visit, and place their first order together.

**Get your salesmen on board.** If your field team sees the app as a threat, they will quietly steer retailers away from it. Show them how it saves them time on order taking, so they can spend that time finding new shops.

**Give a small reason to switch.** A modest discount on app orders, or a scheme that only shows in the app, works better than any amount of persuading.

**Keep WhatsApp, but redirect it.** When an order comes in on chat, reply with the app link and offer to place it for them this once. Within a few weeks most regular buyers will just use the app.

**Watch the numbers.** After a month, check how many of your orders come through the app. If it's under a third, talk to the retailers who aren't using it and ask why. The answer is usually something simple, like a missing product or a confusing pack size.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do my retailers need to be tech savvy to use a B2B ordering app?** No. If they use WhatsApp and UPI, they can use a well-designed ordering app. The first order is the only hard one, which is why it helps to place it with them.

**Can I show different prices to different retailers?** That depends on the platform. Ask to see it in the demo with two test buyers, so you can check what each one sees.

**Will I lose my WhatsApp orders?** No. You can keep taking orders on WhatsApp while retailers move over at their own pace. Most wholesalers run both for the first couple of months.

**Can I use the same app for retail customers too?** Yes, many platforms support B2B and B2C together, so the same store can serve retailers at trade prices and walk-in or online shoppers at retail prices.

**How long does it take to go live?** With a white label platform, usually a week or two. Most of that time goes into adding your products and photos, not the technology.
