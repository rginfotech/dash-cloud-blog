Launching an AI agent is the start, not the finish. Without measurement you cannot tell whether it is saving time, helping customers or quietly making mistakes. Five simple numbers cover most of what matters.

## 1. First response time

How long does a customer wait for the first reply? This should drop sharply after launch. Compare it with the time before the agent.

## 2. Resolution rate

What share of conversations are completed without a person? Track this, but do not chase it blindly. A high number is only good if customers are satisfied.

## 3. Handover rate and reasons

How often does the agent pass a conversation to a human, and why? Reasons point to missing knowledge or unclear rules.

## 4. Customer satisfaction

Ask a quick question at the end of conversations. Even a simple thumbs up or down gives you a trend.

## 5. Business outcome

Choose the result the agent exists for: meetings booked, orders confirmed, quotes sent or leads qualified. This is the number your team cares about most.

## Review conversations, not just charts

Read a sample of real conversations each week. You will find problems no chart reveals, such as wrong tone or outdated information.

## Set a baseline

Record your numbers before launch. Without a baseline, you cannot show improvement. Review monthly and update the agent's instructions based on what you find.

## Be honest about the results

If a metric is not improving, say so and adjust. A transparent review builds trust inside the team.
