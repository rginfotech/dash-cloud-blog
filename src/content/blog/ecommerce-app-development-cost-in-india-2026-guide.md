How much does it cost to build an ecommerce app in India?

If you run a shop, a wholesale business or a small brand, you've probably asked this question to a developer at least once. The answer you got was likely "it depends". That's true, but it doesn't help you plan.

So here is a straight answer. In India, getting your own ecommerce website plus Android and iOS apps costs anywhere from about ₹26,500 in the first year to well over ₹10 lakh. The difference comes down to one decision: do you build from scratch, hire a freelancer, or use a ready-made white label platform?

Below we break down each option, the costs people usually forget, and how to pick the right one for your business.

Option 1: Build a custom app from scratch

This is what most agencies will quote for. A team designs your store, writes the website, the Android app, the iOS app and the admin panel, then hands it over.

Rough market ranges in India for a store with a catalogue, cart, payments, delivery and an admin panel:

Website + admin panel: about ₹2 lakh to ₹5 lakh

Android and iOS apps: about ₹3 lakh to ₹8 lakh for both

Time to launch: usually 4 to 8 months

That's before anything runs. Once it's live you pay for servers, bug fixes, app updates every time Android or iOS changes, and a developer on call when something breaks during a sale. Many businesses end up spending 15–20% of the build cost every year just to keep it running.

When it makes sense: you have a truly unusual business model, an in-house tech team, and the budget to keep paying for it.

Option 2: Hire a freelancer or buy a template

This is the route a lot of small shop owners try first. You find a freelancer, or buy a ready-made ecommerce script or app template, and get someone to set it up with your logo.

Typical costs are much lower up front, roughly ₹30,000 to ₹1.5 lakh. Hosting comes on top, usually a few thousand rupees a month.

The catch shows up later. Templates are built for everyone, so features you need (like COD only for certain pincodes, or wholesale prices for your retailers) often need custom work. If the freelancer moves on, nobody knows the code. And app updates, security patches and payment gateway changes become your problem.

When it makes sense: a very tight budget, a simple catalogue, and someone technical you trust who will stick around.

Costs people forget to budget for

Whichever route you take, these show up sooner or later:

App store accounts. Google Play charges a one-time $25 developer fee. Apple charges $99 every year. You only need these if the apps are published under your own account.

Payment gateway charges. Usually around 2% per online payment, depending on the gateway and payment method.

SMS and WhatsApp messages. Order updates and OTPs are billed per message by the provider.

Delivery. Your own riders, or a shipping partner charging per parcel.

Product photos and catalogue data. Often the slowest part of getting a store live. Good photos sell; blurry ones don't.

Marketing. An app nobody knows about gets no orders. Plan some budget for local ads, WhatsApp broadcasts and offers for the first few months.

Maintenance. With a custom build or a freelancer, every Android or iOS update can mean paid fixes. With a white label plan it's part of the monthly fee.
