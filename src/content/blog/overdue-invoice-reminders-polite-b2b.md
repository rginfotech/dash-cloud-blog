For small suppliers, a late payment is more than an inconvenience. It affects cash flow, planning and sometimes survival. But chasing clients feels awkward, so many businesses delay it for too long.

## Make paying easy first

Many late payments are simply oversight. Clear invoices with the amount, due date, payment details and a contact name solve a lot of problems. Send them promptly.

## A courteous reminder sequence

- **A few days before the due date:** A friendly note with the invoice attached.
- **On the due date:** A short reminder with payment details.
- **A week after:** A polite follow-up asking whether anything is blocking payment.
- **Two weeks after:** A firmer message with a clear request for a date. Offer a call.
- **After that:** A personal call from someone senior, and review your terms.

## Keep the tone professional

Assume good intent. Use clear, calm language and avoid threats. Many clients have approval steps you cannot see, and a respectful tone keeps the relationship intact.

## Where automation helps

An AI agent can send the scheduled reminders, answer questions such as "Can you resend the invoice?" and record promised payment dates. It should stop reminders once payment arrives and pass disputes to a person immediately.

## Know the rules

Late payment terms and collection rules vary by country. This article is general guidance, not legal or financial advice.

## Learn from patterns

If the same clients pay late every time, review your terms, deposits or credit limits. Clear agreements prevent problems better than reminders can.
