In many B2B businesses, the quote is the moment of truth. A buyer sends the same request to several suppliers, and the one who replies first with clear, accurate numbers often wins the conversation.

## Why quotes are slow

- Requests arrive missing key details such as quantity, specification or delivery location
- Someone has to chase for the missing information
- Pricing sits in spreadsheets or in one person's head
- The quote needs approval before it goes out

## Step 1: Collect complete details at the start

When a request arrives, an AI agent can reply immediately and ask for what is missing: product or specification, quantity, delivery location, required date and any certifications. A short, structured set of questions beats a long email thread.

## Step 2: Prepare the quote automatically

Once the details are complete, match them to your price list and rules, such as volume discounts, shipping costs and lead times. Produce a draft quote in your standard format.

## Step 3: Keep a person in control

Let the draft go to a team member for review when:

- The order value is above a set amount
- The product is custom or unusual
- The customer asks for special terms

For standard, low-risk requests, you can allow the quote to go out automatically.

## Step 4: Follow up

Most quotes do not get an instant answer. Schedule a polite follow-up after a few days, and let the agent handle simple questions about delivery or specifications.

## Measure what matters

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| Time from request to quote | The speed buyers notice |
| Share of requests quoted without chasing | Quality of intake |
| Quote-to-order rate | The result that counts |

## A word on accuracy

An incorrect quote costs more than a slow one. Keep your price list current, and test the agent with real past requests before it goes live.
