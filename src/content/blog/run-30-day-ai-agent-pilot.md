Buying AI on promises is risky. A short, well-designed pilot lets you see real results with a small commitment, and gives everyone a fair basis for the decision.

## Step 1: Choose one goal

Pick a single, measurable outcome. Examples: respond to every new enquiry in under five minutes, book more site visits, or confirm more orders before shipping. One goal keeps the pilot honest.

## Step 2: Record your baseline

Before the pilot starts, measure how things work today. Response time, qualified leads per week, bookings, cancellations. Without a baseline, any result is just an opinion.

## Step 3: Agree the success metric in writing

Write down what number counts as a success, and who decides. For example, "at least a 30 percent increase in booked meetings from inbound leads." Set the target realistically, using your own baseline.

## Step 4: Limit the scope

Start with one lead source, one channel and one team. A small scope reduces risk and makes problems easy to spot.

## Step 5: Run it for four weeks

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Set up, test with real examples, fix gaps |
| 2 | Live on the chosen source, review conversations daily |
| 3 | Improve answers and handoff rules |
| 4 | Collect results, compare with the baseline |

## Step 6: Review conversations, not just numbers

Read a sample of real conversations each week. They show where the agent misunderstood, where customers got stuck, and what questions you did not expect.

## Step 7: Decide with data

At the end, compare the results with the success metric. Then choose to scale up, adjust and extend, or stop. Any of these is a good outcome if it is based on evidence.

## What a good pilot agreement includes

- The goal and the metric
- The duration and the price
- Who owns the data, and how it is used and deleted
- What happens at the end

A clear pilot protects both sides, and makes the next step easy.
