Skincare, supplements, coffee, pet food and cleaning supplies all run out. The customer will buy again, the only question is whether they buy from you or from whoever they remember at that moment.

## Work out the usage cycle

Estimate how long one purchase lasts. A 30-day supply should prompt a reminder around day 25. Use your own order data to refine the timing, and note that customers use products at different speeds.

## Make the reminder helpful

A good reminder is short and specific: "Your cleanser should be running low about now. Want to reorder the same size?" Include a direct link or let the customer reply to confirm.

## Let an agent handle the replies

Customers may ask to change the size, delay the order or skip a month. An AI agent can handle these requests, confirm the details and pass anything unusual to your team.

## Offer flexibility

- Let customers snooze the reminder
- Offer a different quantity
- Make unsubscribing easy and respectful

## Avoid common mistakes

Do not send reminders for products that customers rarely buy again, and do not send them too often. If someone has already reordered, stop the reminder.

## Test and learn

Try different timings with small groups. Compare reorder rates and unsubscribes, then adopt the version that works for your customers without annoying them.
