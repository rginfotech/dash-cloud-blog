You spend weeks and a good budget on an event, collect a pile of contacts, and then the follow-up takes two weeks because everyone is back to normal work. By then the buyer has met your competitors too.

## Why event leads go cold

- Contacts sit in notebooks, photos and spreadsheets
- Nobody remembers who showed real interest
- Generic emails get ignored
- Sales and marketing disagree about who owns the follow-up

## A four-step follow-up flow

1. **Capture quickly.** Enter contacts the same day with a short note: topic discussed and level of interest.
2. **Sort.** Mark each as hot, warm or general.
3. **Personalise.** Send a short message within 48 hours that mentions what you talked about.
4. **Hand over.** Hot leads go to a salesperson with the notes. Warm leads receive a short sequence over a few weeks.

## Where AI helps

An agent can draft personalised messages from your notes, send them at the right time, answer first replies and book calls. It can also remind the team when a hot lead has not received a personal response.

## What not to do

Do not add everyone to a newsletter without asking. Respect consent rules in the countries where your contacts live, and make unsubscribing easy.

## Judge by conversations, not contacts

The measure of an event is not how many cards you collected, but how many real conversations and opportunities it produced. Track those from the first week.
