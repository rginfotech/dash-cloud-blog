The most common reason automation disappoints is not the technology. It is that the process underneath was unclear, inconsistent or full of exceptions. Spending an hour on a whiteboard before you start can save weeks later.

## The one-hour exercise

Gather the people who actually do the work, not just the managers. Pick one process, such as handling a new enquiry.

1. **Write each step on a sticky note** in the order it happens today.
2. **Mark who does it** and how long it takes.
3. **Mark the pain points:** delays, repeated work and handoffs.
4. **Note the exceptions:** what happens when something unusual occurs?

## Look for good automation candidates

The best steps are:

- Repeated many times a week
- Based on clear rules
- Slow because of waiting, not thinking
- Low in risk if a mistake happens

## Keep people where judgement matters

Steps that need empathy, negotiation or a difficult decision should stay with a person. Mark them clearly so the agent knows when to hand over.

## Simplify first

Sometimes the right fix is to remove a step. Before automating anything, ask if it is needed at all.

## Turn the map into instructions

A clear map becomes the instructions for your agent: what to ask, what to check and when to escalate. The better the map, the better the results.

## Measure before and after

Record how long the process takes today and how many are handled. You can then show the real effect of your work.
