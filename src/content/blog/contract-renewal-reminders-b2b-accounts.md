Winning a new client takes weeks of effort. Keeping one often takes a single timely conversation. Yet many small businesses let renewals slip, or only discover a problem when the client has already decided to leave.

## Build a renewal timeline

Work backwards from the renewal date:

- **90 days before:** Internal review of the account. How has it gone? Any issues?
- **60 days before:** Contact the client for a check-in and gather feedback.
- **30 days before:** Share the renewal proposal and any updates.
- **14 days before:** Follow up on questions and resolve concerns.
- **After renewal:** Thank the client and confirm the next steps.

## Where automation helps

An AI agent can remind your team of upcoming dates, draft check-in messages from the account history and send routine reminders to clients. It can also flag accounts that have gone quiet.

## Listen for risk signals

Silence, slower replies, fewer orders or complaints are early signs of trouble. Review them regularly and have a person reach out personally.

## Keep conversations two-way

A renewal conversation should be about the client's results and goals, not just the contract. Ask what is working and what is not. Honest answers help you improve.

## Track and improve

Record the renewal rate and the reasons for every lost account. Patterns will show what to fix in your service or product.
