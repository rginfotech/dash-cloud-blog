Many leads do not reply to the first message. That does not mean no. It usually means the message arrived at a busy moment. A short, structured follow-up sequence recovers many of these conversations.

## Why a sequence beats one reminder

People respond at different times. One gentle message at a time, each adding something new, gives prospects several chances to answer without feeling chased.

## A simple schedule

| Touch | When | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| First message | Day 0 | Introduce, ask one clear question |
| Follow-up 1 | Day 3 | Add value: a short example or relevant result |
| Follow-up 2 | Day 7 | Offer help: answer a likely question or objection |
| Follow-up 3 | Day 14 | Polite close: offer to reconnect later |

## Example wording

**Day 3:** "Hi Sam, sharing a short example of how a similar team cut their reply time. Happy to show how it could work for you."

**Day 7:** "Quick check. Is the main question timing, price, or fit? I can send details on any of them."

**Day 14:** "I will pause here so I do not fill your inbox. If this becomes a priority, just reply and we will pick it up."

## Make every touch useful

- Mention something specific to them
- Add one new piece of value each time
- Keep it short
- Give an easy way to say not now

## Use more than one channel

If someone has not replied to email, a short message on another channel they have used can help. Always use only the contact methods the person has shared with you, and honour opt-outs immediately.

## Let automation handle the schedule

Reminders are easy to forget. Scheduled messages, with an AI agent replying when someone answers, keep the sequence going while your team focuses on conversations that are moving forward.

Track replies and meetings booked at each step. If most replies come on Day 7, spend your effort improving that message.
