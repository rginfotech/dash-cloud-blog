New customers trust other customers. A product page with honest, recent reviews usually converts better than one without. The problem is that happy customers rarely write a review unless someone asks.

## Timing is everything

Ask too early and the product has not arrived. Ask too late and the customer has moved on. A good rule is to wait a few days after delivery, long enough to try the product, then send one friendly request.

## A simple review flow

1. **Confirm delivery.** Check that the order arrived.
2. **Ask one easy question.** "How did you find your order?" with a quick rating.
3. **Route by answer.** Happy customers receive a link to leave a public review. Unhappy customers go to your support team.
4. **Say thank you.** A short, genuine message matters.

## Handle unhappy feedback first

A low rating is a chance to fix the problem. When a customer is unhappy, the agent should alert a person, offer help and avoid pushing for a public review. Do not hide or discourage honest reviews. Many marketplaces and platforms have rules on how you may request them, so check the policy of each one.

## Use the words customers use

Reviews show how customers describe your product. Reuse those phrases in your product pages and ads. They are often clearer than marketing language.

## Keep it light

Send one request and at most one gentle reminder. Respect opt-outs. A short, respectful message builds the relationship; repeated nagging damages it.
