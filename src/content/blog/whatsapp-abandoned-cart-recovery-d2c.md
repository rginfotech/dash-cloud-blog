Most online shoppers who add items to a cart do not buy. Some were just comparing prices, but many simply got distracted. A well-timed, helpful message can bring a share of them back.

## Start with consent

Only message customers who have given their number and agreed to receive messages. Follow the WhatsApp Business policy and the privacy rules of each country you sell in. Make opting out one tap.

## A simple three-message sequence

1. **About 1 hour after abandonment:** a friendly reminder with the items and a link back to the cart.
2. **Next day:** answer the likely objection. Shipping time, returns policy or sizing help.
3. **Day 3, optional:** a small incentive, if your margins allow. Stop after this message.

## What a good message looks like

- Uses the customer's name and the product they left
- Is short enough to read in one glance
- Contains one clear button or link
- Offers help, not pressure

## Where AI helps

An AI agent can reply when the customer asks a question, such as "Does this come in blue?" or "When will it arrive?". It can answer from your catalogue and shipping policy, then send the checkout link again. A fixed template cannot do that.

## What to measure

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| Reply rate | Is the message relevant? |
| Recovered orders | The result that matters |
| Opt-out rate | A warning sign if messages are too frequent |
| Revenue per message sent | Compares cost against return |

## Common mistakes

- Messaging every visitor, not only people who showed clear intent
- Sending more than three messages
- Offering a discount to everyone, which trains customers to abandon carts on purpose
- Ignoring replies

Test the sequence on one product category first. Keep what works and drop what does not.
